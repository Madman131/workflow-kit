# Legacy handoff replay compatibility contract

Tier: T2. Candidate base: `588593e031f822ed31df6deab1837651b1de9180`.
Build scope: one bounded replay correction in `hooks/repair-dispatch-state.mjs`, regression tests, and a minimal decision/receipt. No standard-ledger rewrite, public-recorder relaxation, tier change, or runtime/trust change.

## Mechanism and provenance

A `legacy_handoff` row in the aggregate ledger cites a standard parent by `parent_task_id` and `parent_changeset_id`. At the row's sequence position, replay derives that parent's active latest standard disposition and validates its exact event ID, round, candidate digest, and authorized paths before creating child lineage. The outer aggregate `task_id` and `changeset_id` are envelope IDs; supported historical grammar did not require them to equal the cited parent IDs. This applies to absent policy version (policy 1) and explicit policies 2, 3, **and 4**. Raw source: `588593e^:hooks/repair-dispatch-state.mjs` lacks the equality predicates in both `recordAggregateLegacyHandoff` and `aggregateWorld`; `588593e:hooks/repair-dispatch-state.mjs` adds them to both. The parent commit already declares policy 4. No trustworthy row timestamp or provenance flag distinguishes policy 4 rows recorded before and after that change. Forbidding policy 4 during replay would discard valid older records. Parent authority still comes from the cited standard row; under the old recorder, an unrelated historical T3 outer program could supply a policy 3 envelope for an Owner-evidenced T3 child. Already-recorded T3 lineage keeps its original T3 obligations. This does not authorize the current recorder to mint a new T3 child.

The *current public recorder* must continue rejecting new separate-ID requests with its exact outer-ID equality checks. Replay accepts a historical row with separate outer IDs only when all other legacy handoff guards hold. The replay condition must not infer an invented cutoff from policy version, timestamp, session, or digest. A fully well-formed out-of-band planted row remains within the ledger's existing records-not-authenticated-provenance residual; no local predicate can distinguish it from an authentic historical row of the same bytes.

## Falsifiable acceptance criteria

1. For each supported policy grammar (absent/1, 2, 3, 4), build a raw historical standard parent and a hash-valid Owner-evidenced separate-ID `legacy_handoff` tied to its active latest disposition at that ledger position. The aggregate child opens a panel, reaches STOP, reserves its paths, and keeps the standard parent retired from the moment the handoff is accepted; a later standard close is administrative cleanup. Exercise both a policy 4 witness and a policy 2 witness through that full lifecycle. A policy 4 replay refusal is a regression, even if the current recorder refuses the same input.
2. For established policies 2, 3, and 4, at a required review checkpoint (next gate ordinal divisible by four), replay accepts a separate-ID historical handoff only with a permitted typed `legacy_handoff` review bound to the standard parent, exact anchor, ordinal, and transition digest. A missing review, wrong parent, wrong anchor, wrong transition, or unpermitted cited ruling cannot authorize lineage. The old public recorder could append a policy 4 successor-cited handoff after an applicable policy 2 `owner_decision` review; replay preserves that child because the ruling was machine-permitted and its prose cannot be decoded as a hold. A sole exact cited `owner_decision` with Owner evidence remains permitted. The current recorder conservatively refuses a newly proposed handoff that cites one review while leaving an applicable `owner_decision` uncited, with no append. This covers the `applicableTypedReviews` behavior added at `588593e`. Policy 1 preserves its older optional `process_review_event_id` grammar; replay does not invent a typed-review requirement for a policy 1 row that lacks that field. Standard `round_disposition` parents have no aggregate policy version to inherit or downgrade.
3. The current public recorder rejects a new separate-ID handoff, including a cross-envelope T3 request, and leaves the ledger unchanged. A normal same-ID current T2 handoff still records and replays, with its ordinary review behavior intact. An already-recorded policy 3 separate-ID T3 child remains pending and can open and close only at T3 strength, with its Owner and review obligations intact.
4. Replay refuses malformed or conflicting historical rows: missing Owner evidence, inactive or stale parent, wrong parent changeset, wrong latest event/round/candidate/paths, invalid child shape or tier under policy 4, child task/changeset collision, overlap with active or pending lineage, and overlap with a STOP reservation. It must not rewrite an existing STOP or let a later standard row retroactively alter a prior valid handoff.
5. A separate-ID handoff takes neither admission prefilters nor accepted-row policy promotion from an unrelated aggregate program named by the outer envelope. With an already-open policy 4 outer program, a hash-valid policy 2 handoff still reaches its cited standard parent and child checks; the corresponding historical recorder provenance for that exact ordering is unproven. Conversely, an accepted policy 4 handoff cannot promote an unrelated policy 2 program; that program’s next valid historical panel close and continuation remain accepted. Disabled isolation arms must lose the corresponding child or continuation. Existing finite-round, terminality, path ownership, and aggregate/standard collision guards remain effective. No test passes merely by checking that a row parses; witnesses assert derived lineage and subsequent admitted or refused operations.

Threat model: deterministic ledger replay of supported historical rows, including malformed, stale, conflicting, or out-of-band inserted rows. Residual: a fully well-formed forged row matching all authority evidence is indistinguishable from an authentic historical record because the ledger authenticates record shape and chain, not age or authorship. Review prose has no reliable polarity bit, so replay cannot infer that an uncited machine-permitted `owner_decision` meant a negative hold. Mixed old-writer activation remains an operational hold outside this replay correction. Crash/retry behavior belongs to the existing idempotent append path and is not changed here; empty-tick behavior does not apply to deterministic replay.
