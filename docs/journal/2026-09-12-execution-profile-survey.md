### Important qualifications

- Terra really has been writing significant product code; this was not merely a plan to delegate.
  It is not established that Terra wrote every line. Parent tasks also edited declarations,
  contracts, evidence, and temporary scripts, and some repair dispatches inherited the parent model.
- Names are not model evidence: `terra_cleanup_diagnosis` was dispatched without an explicit model,
  and its own turn metadata confirms `gpt-5.6-sol`. A different dispatch named
  `astra_brain_permit_activation_decision` also omitted the model override. Do not credit an Astra
  consultation merely because the task name says Astra.
- Luna was used in several review seats as well as bulk work. That is observed practice, not a
  recommended review floor; the existing workhorse-minimum gate policy must not be weakened by
  interpreting the new Luna gathering role as general gate authority.
- Several review transmissions were blocked by host/private-export controls. These are different
  from a repository instruction unnecessarily asking for permission. A repository policy cannot
  bypass host safeguards, silently replace a required reviewer, or claim an undelivered gate ran.
- Product runtime roles (for example, Luna gathering inside a live Trader run) are distinct from
  the models building that product. This proposed build profile does not change runtime models,
  schedules, subscriptions, API spending, live permits, or data permissions.

## Owner decisions to integrate

### Roles and continuity

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Owner + Astra, planning phase | Agree intent, scope, acceptance evidence, risk and the implementation plan. Owner approves the plan and consequential tradeoffs. |
| Sol, implementation PM/orchestrator | Own the ongoing implementation conversation, sequencing, worker briefs, gate administration, finding decisions, bounded repair choices, integration and reporting. Consult Astra at the defined triggers; do not keep Astra as the standing build conversation. |
| Terra, code builder | Write product code and tests, perform code repairs, and maintain the complete source context for the assigned changeset. One active author per overlapping file set. |
| Luna, gathering/bulk worker | Locate, extract, inventory and organize evidence and perform well-specified bulk work. Return source pointers and uncertainties; do not make architectural decisions, author product-code fixes, or approve gates merely because it is cheaper. |
| Astra, implementation consultant | Review a compact, source-backed packet for critical ambiguity, proposed design changes, loss of direction, root-cause escalation and mandatory process checkpoints. A critical design change still returns to the Owner. |

The planning-to-implementation handoff must identify the approved artifact, acceptance evidence,
exclusions, risk, model assignments, existing gate state and Owner boundaries. It preserves review
history and does not restart counts. A phase change must verify the actual active model; neither a
prompt nor a friendly worker name switches a model. Use concrete dated bindings, small source-backed
briefs, existing worker context when suitable, and deterministic checks before model review. Do not
save tokens by dropping required reviewers or substituting a weaker decision-maker silently.

Reconcile the retention guarantee around the **designated Terra Builder's conversation**, rather
than prohibiting all higher-risk delegated implementation. Sol verifies the raw changed artifacts
and decisive evidence; a worker summary alone cannot discharge that responsibility. This is a
method amendment, not permission to relabel a retired lane or defeat its hooks.

### Routine authority belongs to Sol

Within the approved plan, existing access and agreed budget, Sol may proceed without repeated
Owner confirmation through inspection, worker dispatch, tests, local commits, eligible local
integration/merges, required review calls, Gemini packet/slice planning, permitted Claude/Gemini
review transmissions, finding decisions, and one permitted repair batch at a time. Review
transmissions contain only the in-scope artifact and necessary review context, with secrets, live
data and unrelated material excluded.

Sol may draft, link and prioritize follow-up tickets. Creating a ticket does not authorize new
scope, reopen a terminal parent or grant a fresh repair allowance. Routine implementation choices
inside the approved design do not require a new Owner decision. Local integration means neither
remote PR merge nor any action that pushes, deploys, activates, or performs a live write.

Owner decisions remain: initial plan approval; fresh push/deploy authorization; required named live
write/activation authorization; critical design or intent changes; material scope, cost or budget
changes; new credentials or destructive/irreversible acts; and unresolved terminal/recursive-gating
decisions. Approval should name a bounded operation and cover its normal dependent steps; do not
ask again for the same already-authorized act. Report unexpected boundary changes explicitly.

At plan approval, capture risk/tier and the scope of local integration and review transmission
together so the Owner does not repeatedly ratify unchanged facts. A changed risk or critical
design is a new decision, not something Sol may hide inside a routine repair. Host-required login,
trust and permission interactions remain human steps; report the specific barrier, not a generic
request to continue.

### Keep the finite review controller

One aggregate round collects the required panel against one frozen candidate. Reviewer seats,
Gemini slices, tests and provider transport retries are not additional aggregate rounds.

Close early when the required review is complete and no accepted blocker remains; three rounds
are not compulsory. Preserve the existing Round 2 root-cause/design consultation triggers. Before
the transition into Round 4, obtain the required fresh Astra process review. Round 4 is the final
GO/STOP bookend for that changeset; no Round 5, cycle reset, or renamed continuation of the parent.
Cumulative checkpoints 4/8/12 across authorized descendants remain and confer no extra round.
After terminal STOP, Sol recommends a narrow successor or abandonment; the Owner decides. A small
remaining defect can justify a **bounded completion exception**: a fresh Astra zoom-out and Sol
recommendation name the concrete harm, smallest correction, fixed scope and completion proof. The
Owner approves once, then Sol runs one repair batch and the required final review without routine
permission requests. Use the existing parent-linked successor mechanism; keep the parent closed,
retain cumulative history and do not grant a fresh four-round budget. Failure or expansion stops
the exception; no automatic repeat, extension or “Cycle 2.” Required review depth remains intact.
This is the recursive-gating intervention the Owner retained, not a routine permission request.

The observed Trader Round 5 had Owner authorization. Its existence alone does not establish that
continuing was inappropriate; a bounded extra finish can be justified. The approved policy limits
that finish explicitly and prevents serial extensions from becoming the default process.

- Adoption ticket: configure and demonstrate the profile, review routes and approval boundaries.
  Factsheet: update the affected roles/setup explanations while preserving the Owner's formatting.

Before treating this as shipped method, review the wording and existing control implications. Test
representative decisions: routine review transmits without another Owner ask; critical design does
ask; Terra owns a T2 code change without competing authors; an inherited Sol task is not counted as
Astra; three reviewers are one round; Round 4 cannot grant Round 5; drafting a successor cannot start
it; push approval never becomes permission for an unapproved live write. Prefer existing mechanisms
and focused checks; add no scheduler, general policy engine or new ledger for this amendment.
